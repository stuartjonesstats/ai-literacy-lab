# Module 1: What AI Is Good and Bad At

## Durable Question

When is AI useful, and when is it fragile?

## Learning Goal

Learners will classify potential AI uses by considering task type, context, stakes, data sensitivity, verification options, and human accountability.

## Opening Scenario

You are preparing for a busy workday. Several people have suggested using AI to save time.

You see the following possible uses:

1. Rewrite a routine announcement so it is clearer.
2. Summarize a long internal document that may contain confidential information.
3. Draft a performance review for an employee.
4. Brainstorm titles for a public presentation.
5. Identify the correct policy that applies to a customer complaint.
6. Generate code for a small internal spreadsheet automation.
7. Decide which job applicants should advance to interviews.
8. Create a checklist for planning a team meeting.

## First Move

Sort each use into one of three categories:

- Usually reasonable with review
- Maybe, but needs constraints or safeguards
- High risk or inappropriate without formal review

Do not use a generic rule like "AI is good for writing" or "AI is bad for decisions." Explain what changes your answer.

## Complication

Now imagine that each task happens in a different context:

- The announcement includes details about a future reorganization.
- The presentation title is for a sensitive public health campaign.
- The customer complaint involves a regulatory deadline.
- The spreadsheet automation will be used by twenty other people.
- The meeting checklist is for a routine internal planning session.

Revise your classifications.

## Concept

AI suitability is not only about the task category. It depends on context.

Useful questions:

- What is the intended use?
- What happens if the output is wrong?
- Can a human verify the output?
- Is sensitive data involved?
- Does the output affect someone else's opportunity, rights, safety, finances, or reputation?
- Who remains accountable?

## Second Attempt

Choose one task you initially classified too casually. Redesign the workflow so AI can be used more responsibly, or explain why it should not be used.

## Rubric

- Emerging: sorts tasks based mostly on whether AI seems generally capable.
- Developing: identifies obvious high-stakes or sensitive-data cases.
- Proficient: explains how context, stakes, verification, and accountability change the classification.
- Advanced: redesigns the workflow to reduce risk while preserving useful AI assistance.

## Transfer Principle

AI is often useful for generating, transforming, organizing, and critiquing information. It is more fragile when work requires current facts, hidden context, sensitive data, high-stakes judgment, or accountable decisions.
