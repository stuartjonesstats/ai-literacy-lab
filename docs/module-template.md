## 1. Opening Scenario

Describe a realistic, generic situation. Keep it concrete enough to reason about, but not tied to a single employer, vendor, product, law, or year.

The scenario should create a decision, not merely introduce a topic.

## 2. First Move

Ask the learner to make a choice, classification, prediction, or short recommendation before receiving the full explanation.

Examples:

- Would you use AI here?
- What risk level is this?
- What evidence would you need before relying on this?
- What part of the output would you verify first?
- Who should review this before it is used?

## 3. Artifact

Provide something for the learner to inspect.

Possible artifacts:

- AI-generated output
- workplace email
- draft policy
- synthetic dataset excerpt
- meeting notes
- customer message
- manager request
- workflow diagram
- risk register entry

## 4. Complication

Reveal information that makes the first answer incomplete.

The complication should create productive struggle. It should not depend on obscure technical knowledge.

## 5. Concept Naming

Introduce the key concept after the learner has encountered the problem.

Keep the explanation short and transferable.

## 6. Second Attempt

Give the learner a similar but not identical task so they can apply the concept immediately.

## 7. Debrief

Help the learner compare their initial reasoning with their revised reasoning.

Useful prompts:

- What did you notice after the complication?
- What would you verify before acting?
- What would you document?
- What would make this use lower risk?
- What would require escalation?

## 8. Rubric

Define what strong evidence of learning looks like.

Example scale:

- Emerging: identifies the obvious issue but misses context or stakes.
- Developing: identifies the main risk and one reasonable mitigation.
- Proficient: explains the risk, evidence gap, review need, and decision boundary.
- Advanced: anticipates second-order consequences and adapts the workflow.

## 9. Transfer

Name the broader principle and where it applies outside the scenario.
