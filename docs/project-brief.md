# Project Brief

## Working Name

AI Literacy Lab

## One-Sentence Description

An open-source curriculum for building practical AI judgment through realistic scenarios, productive struggle, and transferable decision habits.

## Core Thesis

AI literacy is the ability to make responsible, context-aware decisions about when, how, and whether to use AI systems.

## North Star

This should feel like a practice-based AI literacy lab, not a slide-based awareness course.

Learners should encounter realistic ambiguity, make imperfect decisions, receive structured feedback, revise their reasoning, and leave with stronger habits for responsible AI use across tools, roles, and eras.

## Primary Audience

Broad professional and adult-learning audiences:

- individual learners
- teams adopting AI tools
- educators and trainers
- public sector staff
- nonprofit staff
- managers
- analysts
- operations workers
- organizations that need AI literacy without heavy technical prerequisites

The material should have a low technical floor and a high judgment ceiling.

## Product Principles

- Free and open by default.
- Built around scenarios and artifacts, not slides.
- Generic enough for many sectors.
- Durable enough to survive changing AI products.
- Serious about governance without becoming legal advice.
- Useful for self-guided learning and facilitated workshops.
- Easy to fork, adapt, cite, and contribute to.

## What Makes It Different

Most AI literacy training teaches awareness. This project should teach discernment.

The signature move is to make learners experience the gap between "this output looks good" and "this output is justified, safe, appropriate, and accountable."

## Future Delivery Modes

- Open curriculum on GitHub
- Public static website linked from personal site and LinkedIn
- Self-guided learning path
- Team discussion path
- Facilitated workshop formats
- Train-the-facilitator materials
