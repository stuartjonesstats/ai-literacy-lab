# Organizational Adoption Guide

AI Literacy Lab is designed to be useful in offices without becoming a policy
system. It can help employees practice judgment, but it does not replace local
governance.

## Appropriate Uses

- self-guided AI literacy practice
- onboarding supplement for general AI use expectations
- team discussion about AI risk, data boundaries, and review
- workshop backbone for managers, analysts, operations staff, and public-sector teams
- pre-work before a policy rollout or tool launch

## Not Appropriate As

- a proctored credential
- proof of legal or regulatory compliance
- approval to use a particular AI tool
- replacement for data classification, privacy, security, procurement, HR, or legal review
- surveillance of learner reflections or written responses

## Recommended Adoption Pattern

1. Link the lab from an internal AI guidance page.
2. Tell learners which internal AI tools are approved and for what data.
3. Pair the lab with your organization's escalation contacts.
4. Ask teams to discuss one module together, not just complete it alone.
5. Use the final learning record as evidence of practice, not certification.

## Readiness Checklist

Before scheduling a facilitated session, confirm:

- audience, required outcome, and whether completion is optional or required
- approved tools, prohibited uses, and data classification examples
- escalation contacts for privacy, security, legal, HR, compliance, procurement,
  and tool support
- completion handling, including submission route, retention, and access
- realistic local examples that do not expose sensitive data
- facilitator preview, learner access, printing, and local storage behavior
- an alternate attestation route if browser storage or printing is unavailable

## Local Policy Addendum Template

Before assigning the lab, prepare a short local addendum:

- audience and scope
- approved AI tools, account requirements, and disabled features
- prohibited inputs, including confidential, regulated, identifying,
  contractual, security, or personnel data
- high-risk uses involving rights, access, safety, money, employment, or legal
  obligations
- required review for facts, sources, records, and external communications
- escalation contacts for privacy, security, legal, HR, compliance,
  procurement, and tool support
- completion handling, including whether the lab is optional, recommended, or
  required internally
- policy owner, effective date, and review cadence

## Facilitated Deployment

Use one of two patterns:

- ask learners to complete the full sequenced lab beforehand, then discuss
  Modules 1, 2, 3, and 7 together; or
- use facilitator preview during the session to open selected modules for group
  discussion without treating that preview as learner completion.

Suggested run sheets:

- 60 minutes: frame local guardrails, discuss Modules 1, 2, and 7, then capture
  one workflow that needs clearer guidance.
- 90 minutes: add Module 3 and a small-group rewrite of one local workflow with
  review and escalation steps.
- 180 minutes: guide Modules 1, 2, 3, 5, and 7 with two short breaks and a final
  list of policy gaps.
- Half-day: guide Modules 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, and the capstone memo, then confirm
  follow-up owners for unresolved questions.

In every session, discuss:

- where learners changed their minds after a complication
- which examples resemble local work
- what should be escalated locally
- where current policy is unclear
- how managers should model responsible use

## Misconception Bank

Common misconceptions and facilitator moves:

- "AI is objective." Ask what data, incentives, omissions, and review steps shape
  the output.
- "Confident prose means verified work." Require checks for dates, numbers,
  names, citations, and commitments.
- "Public information is always safe to paste." Separate public facts from
  confidential context, identifiers, and internal intent.
- "A draft cannot cause harm." Track where drafts influence decisions, records,
  messages, or downstream automation.
- "Human review is a quick skim." Name the accountable reviewer and the standard
  they must apply.
- "Escalation slows the work." Treat escalation as routing uncertainty to the
  right owner before impact grows.
- "Lab completion approves tool use." Clarify that approval comes from local
  policy, not the curriculum.

## Evidence and Privacy

The public site is no-login and local-first. Learner progress, reflections, and
the final learning record are stored in the browser when local storage is
available. They are not submitted to a server by the public version.

Organizations that need internally verifiable completion should create a
separate local process, such as asking learners to submit the printed learning
record through an existing learning system. Do not silently collect learner
writing from the public version.

Before requiring submission, tell learners what to submit, who can see it, how
long it is retained, and whether reflections are reviewed. Provide an alternate
attestation route for learners whose browsers cannot retain local progress.

## Quality Bar

When adapting the lab, keep these constraints:

- use synthetic examples unless rights and privacy are clear
- cite primary or reputable sources for factual claims
- label jurisdiction-specific guidance clearly
- avoid vendor marketing
- preserve scenario-first practice and revision pressure
- avoid turning the lab into slide reading and recall quizzes
