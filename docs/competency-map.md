# AI Literacy Competency Map

This map defines what the curriculum should help learners practice. It is intentionally tool-agnostic.

## 1. Capability Judgment

Learners can explain what kinds of tasks AI systems may help with and what kinds of tasks require caution.

Observable behaviors:

- distinguishes drafting from deciding
- separates brainstorming from verification
- recognizes when context is missing
- avoids treating fluency as proof of correctness

## 2. Verification and Evidence

Learners can decide what needs to be checked before AI-assisted work is used.

Observable behaviors:

- identifies unsupported claims
- checks high-consequence facts
- asks what source, evidence, or review is needed
- notices false precision and overconfident language

## 3. Data Boundaries

Learners can classify information sensitivity and choose safer alternatives.

Observable behaviors:

- avoids entering confidential, personal, proprietary, or regulated information into inappropriate systems
- redacts or abstracts examples where possible
- considers whether AI use creates new records or disclosures
- asks whether the tool, policy, and context permit the use

## 4. Bias and Harm Awareness

Learners can identify ways AI use may affect people unevenly or unfairly.

Observable behaviors:

- considers who is represented and who is missing
- notices proxy variables and stereotypes
- questions apparently neutral outputs when stakes are high
- asks who may be harmed by error or misuse

## 5. Accountability

Learners understand that AI assistance does not remove human responsibility.

Observable behaviors:

- identifies who owns the final decision
- distinguishes review from rubber-stamping
- documents assumptions and limitations
- keeps humans responsible for consequential choices

## 6. Workflow Design

Learners can use AI as one part of a responsible work process.

Observable behaviors:

- defines the purpose before using AI
- gives appropriate constraints
- separates generation, critique, verification, and final decision
- uses AI to augment thinking rather than bypass it

## 7. Risk Classification and Escalation

Learners can decide when an AI use should proceed, be modified, be reviewed, or be escalated.

Observable behaviors:

- considers consequence, reversibility, data sensitivity, affected people, and verification burden
- explains borderline cases
- escalates when policy, stakes, or uncertainty require it
- proposes mitigations proportional to risk

## 8. Reflective Practice

Learners can revise their own habits as AI systems and work contexts change.

Observable behaviors:

- updates decisions when new information appears
- notices automation bias in themselves and others
- articulates what changed in their reasoning
- transfers principles to unfamiliar tools or scenarios
