# Governance

AI Literacy Lab is curated. The goal is not to collect prompts or opinions; the
goal is to maintain a durable, source-aware learning environment for practical
AI judgment.

## Content Governance

Curriculum changes should preserve:

- scenario-first learning
- productive struggle without humiliation
- generic office applicability
- synthetic examples unless rights and privacy are clear
- transparent local checks rather than opaque grading
- clear source notes for factual claims
- caveats where a topic touches legal, privacy, HR, security, or compliance risk

## Source Standard

Factual claims should be supported by primary sources, reputable standards,
peer-reviewed research, or clearly labeled as design heuristics. Broad
framework citations should explain what the source supports and what it does
not prove.

## Review Expectations

Before public release of a substantive content change:

- check module claims against the relevant `research/` notes
- run the site build
- review accessibility impacts of new interactions
- confirm that no learner writing is silently submitted
- avoid vendor-specific or time-bound claims unless clearly labeled

## Maintainer Role

Maintainers decide whether proposed changes fit the project's learning model,
tone, source standard, and privacy posture. Maintainers may reject otherwise
useful material if it turns the lab into a policy manual, prompt collection,
vendor showcase, or slide-based compliance course.
