# Internal Sources for Module 5

This file records the accuracy posture behind Module 5.

## Claim

Human review is only meaningful when the human has enough context, evidence, authority, time, and independence to change the outcome.

## Learner Move

Open source evidence when consequence is nontrivial, avoid relying on confidence cues, check authority, document uncertainty, and escalate when review conditions are inadequate.

## Source Anchors

- NIST AI RMF 1.0 frames AI governance and risk management around context, accountability, validity, reliability, transparency, and impacts.
  - https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-ai-rmf-10
- NIST AI RMF Generative AI Profile is a cross-sectoral companion resource for generative AI risk management.
  - https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-generative-artificial-intelligence
- Automation bias research describes overreliance on automated decision support and reduced vigilance in information seeking and processing.
  - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7651899/

## Caveat

This module does not claim that human review automatically satisfies legal, ethical, or governance requirements. It teaches a durable review-quality habit using synthetic cases.

## Scenario Accuracy

The cases are synthetic. Recommended actions are based only on supplied facts and the stated learning goal: distinguishing nominal review from meaningful review.
