# Module 1 Source Note: What AI Is Good and Bad At

## Durable Claim

AI suitability depends on context, consequences, data sensitivity,
verification, affected people, and accountability. It is not determined by a
static list of "good" or "bad" task categories.

## Source Anchors

- NIST AI RMF 1.0 emphasizes mapping the use context and managing risks based
  on impacts, not treating AI risk as context-free.
  - https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-ai-rmf-10
- NIST AI RMF Generative AI Profile identifies information integrity, privacy,
  bias, and human oversight as relevant risk areas for generative AI use.
  - https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-generative-artificial-intelligence

## Learner Move

Learners classify workplace AI uses by asking what can go wrong, who is
affected, what data is involved, whether the output can be checked, and who owns
the final decision.

## Caveat

The module does not claim that a task type is always safe or always unsafe. The
examples are synthetic and designed to teach a transferable classification
habit.
