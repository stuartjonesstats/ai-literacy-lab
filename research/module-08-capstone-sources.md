# Module 8 Source Note: Workplace AI Decision Simulation

## Durable Claim

Responsible AI use requires integrating capability judgment, evidence checking,
data boundaries, fairness and harm analysis, human accountability, workflow
design, and escalation.

## Source Anchors

- NIST AI RMF 1.0 frames AI risk management as a socio-technical practice that
  includes mapping context, measuring risks, managing risks, and governing
  accountability.
  - https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-ai-rmf-10
- NIST AI RMF Generative AI Profile identifies risks associated with generated
  content, information integrity, privacy, bias, and human oversight.
  - https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-generative-artificial-intelligence
- NIST Privacy Framework supports privacy risk management practices such as
  identifying data processing context and controlling data use.
  - https://www.nist.gov/privacy-framework

## Learner Move

Learners must turn a realistic but synthetic workplace request into a defensible
recommendation. They should decide what AI can assist with, inspect generated
claims against supplied evidence, limit sensitive data exposure, name possible
representational harms, require human review with authority, and define
escalation triggers.

## Caveat

This capstone does not teach a universal legal approval rule, customer service
policy, or regulatory risk tier. It uses synthetic facts to test transferable
judgment under uncertainty.
